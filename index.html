Websites & landing pages

A fast, clear site that explains what you do and turns visitors into inquiries.

From $600

A site whose only job is to explain what you do and turn a visitor into an inquiry.

Where it starts

Usually one of two things. There is no site at all and you are losing the people who looked you up, or there is a site a relative built years ago that you cannot edit and that no longer says what you actually do.

How it gets built

  1. One interview, about an hour. I ask how you would explain the job to a neighbour.
  2. I write the copy from that. You read it as a document before anything gets designed.
  3. Design and build. You review real screens in a browser, not pictures of screens.
  4. Launch, then a walkthrough where you edit a page yourself while I watch.

What moves the price

From $600 is the floor. These are what push a real quote away from it.

  • More pages — three is the floor, ten is a different build
  • Booking, payment or a quote calculator inside the page
  • Several locations or service areas that each need to rank
  • You already have photos and a logo
  • You write your own copy, or approve mine quickly
The technical bit

Hand-built HTML, CSS and JavaScript when the site is mostly stable, or a lightweight CMS when you need to change things weekly. Static hosting behind a CDN so pages arrive fast anywhere. DNS and SSL set up and handed over to you. Forms post to your inbox and your phone at the same time, with a stored copy so nothing depends on an email arriving.

AI systems & automations

The quiet machinery that answers, sorts, follows up and records while you work.

From $1,200

The quiet machinery that answers, sorts, follows up and records while you are on a job.

Where it starts

One task that eats hours every week and that you could describe in a single sentence. Missed calls. Chasing quotes. Re-typing the same job details into three different places.

How it gets built

  1. We map what actually happens now, step by step, including the parts you do without thinking.
  2. We pick one workflow. Not five — the first has to work before it earns the second.
  3. I build it and run it alongside your manual process, so nothing depends on it yet.
  4. Once it has matched your own handling for a week or two, we cut over.

What moves the price

From $1,200 is the floor. These are what push a real quote away from it.

  • How many tools have to talk to each other
  • A tool with no proper API, which means a workaround
  • More decision branches — “if it is a commercial job, do this instead”
  • Your tools are mainstream and already connect
  • One clean workflow rather than a rebuild of everything
The technical bit

n8n, Make or Zapier depending on how complex the logic is and who maintains it afterwards. Claude or OpenAI APIs for any step that has to read or write language — reading an inquiry, drafting a reply, summarising a week. Webhooks between the tools you already pay for. Every run is logged, so when something looks wrong you can see exactly what the system did and why.

Chatbots & support AI

An assistant on your site and phone that answers real questions, not scripts.

From $900

An assistant on your site and your phone that answers real questions instead of reciting a script.

Where it starts

You are answering the same twenty questions every week. Do you cover my area, what does it cost, how soon can you come, do you handle emergencies.

How it gets built

  1. We collect what you already have — prices, service area, FAQs, past emails.
  2. I ground the assistant in that material so it answers from your facts rather than guessing.
  3. We test it against questions you have actually been asked, and fix what it gets wrong.
  4. We set the handoff rules: what it answers, what it books, and what goes straight to you.

What moves the price

From $900 is the floor. These are what push a real quote away from it.

  • It books or quotes, rather than only answering
  • Phone coverage as well as the website
  • Little existing written material, so the source has to be created
  • You already have prices and FAQs written down
  • Website only, answering only
The technical bit

Retrieval over your own documents rather than a general model improvising — it can only answer from material you gave it. Claude or OpenAI for the language itself. For phone, a telephony provider handles the call and the same assistant answers it. Anything it is not confident about escalates to you with the whole conversation attached.

Branding & graphics

A mark, a palette and a template set that make a small operation look established.

From $400

A mark, a palette and a template set that make a one-van operation look established.

Where it starts

You look smaller than you are. The logo is a font somebody picked in 2016, and the truck, the invoice and the Instagram look like three different companies.

How it gets built

  1. A short interview about who you want to be mistaken for, and who you do not.
  2. Mark, colour palette and type, presented as a small set of options rather than forty.
  3. A template set for the things you actually send — quotes, invoices, posts, signage.
  4. You get the source files. They are yours, not licensed from me.

What moves the price

From $400 is the floor. These are what push a real quote away from it.

  • A full mark from scratch rather than a cleanup of what you have
  • Print and vehicle work, which needs different files and specs
  • More templates
  • You keep your existing name treatment and refresh only colour and type
  • Digital only
The technical bit

Vector source files, so the mark works on a business card and on a truck door. A written palette and type spec, so a printer and a website produce the same colour. Templates in Canva or Figma so you can change the wording yourself without opening a design tool you do not know.

Content systems

A repeatable way to publish without it landing on your evenings.

From $600

A repeatable way to publish that does not land on your evenings.

Where it starts

You know you should be posting. You post for two weeks, a busy month happens, and it stops. The problem is not discipline. There is no system underneath it.

How it gets built

  1. We pick the one or two channels that make sense for your customers, and drop the rest.
  2. We build a format you can repeat — the same shapes of post, not a blank page every time.
  3. I set up a queue, so things are scheduled ahead rather than made the morning they go out.
  4. We run one full month together, then you take it over with the system still standing.

What moves the price

From $600 is the floor. These are what push a real quote away from it.

  • Video rather than static posts
  • More channels
  • I produce the material rather than assembling what you shoot
  • You are comfortable filming rough clips on a phone
  • One channel, done properly
The technical bit

A scheduling tool holding the queue, so a week of posting takes one sitting. AI-assisted repurposing turns one asset into several formats — a job walkthrough becomes a short, a carousel and a caption. An asset library so photos from real jobs are findable months later. Nothing publishes without passing your approval step.

AI training for your team

A half-day that turns “I don't get it” into “I use this every day.”

Where it starts

Everyone tells you to use AI. Nobody tells you which part, on what, or what it is bad at. So it gets opened once and never again.

How it gets built

  3. We build at least one thing that is still useful on Monday.
  4. You get a written cheat sheet in your own words, not a generic handout.

What moves the price

From $250 is the floor. These are what push a real quote away from it.

  • A larger team, or separate sessions for different roles
  • On-site rather than remote
  • We build a working tool during the session and keep it
  • Remote
  • A small team in one room
The technical bit

Whatever you will actually keep paying for — usually Claude or ChatGPT on your own accounts, not mine. Prompt patterns written around your real tasks. A short written guide to what should never go into a chatbot: customer records, card details, anything you would not email to a stranger.

How billing works

Fifty percent to start, fifty on launch. Nothing is invoiced before you approve a written scope.

Fixed price, not hourly. If a build takes longer than I estimated, that's my problem, not your invoice.

You own everything at the end — the site, the accounts, the automations and the documentation.

Third-party costs (hosting, AI usage, phone numbers) are passed through at cost, typically $20–80 a month.

The monthly is optional. On your own accounts you keep every subscription and login, I keep it running, and you can drop me whenever you like. Hosted, it runs on my infrastructure — you never see a subscription and I own the uptime, which is what the higher figure pays for.

Either way the build is yours. Nothing is held hostage if you stop the monthly.
